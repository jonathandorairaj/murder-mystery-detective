# The Mysterious Affair at Styles Court

## Detective Briefing

**Location:** Styles Court, a large country estate near the village of Styles St. Mary, Essex
**Date of Death:** Tuesday, 17 July 1920
**Victim:** Mrs. Emily Cavendish-Inglethorp, aged 70, wealthy widow and mistress of Styles Court

---

## The Scene

Styles Court is a handsome Elizabethan manor house set in wooded grounds several miles from the nearest town of Tadminster. Emily Cavendish-Inglethorp was the owner and undisputed mistress of the estate, having inherited it through her first husband. She was a vigorous, generous woman who supported a large household: her two stepsons John and Lawrence Cavendish, John's wife Mary, and until recently her companion of many years, Evelyn Howard.

Two months ago, Emily astonished the household by marrying Alfred Inglethorp, a man twenty years her junior, theatrical in manner and dress, with a black pointed beard and flamboyant waistcoats. The match caused immediate friction. Evelyn Howard left the house in protest. John Cavendish made no secret of his displeasure.

---

## Discovery of the Body

At approximately 2:15 AM on the night of 17 July, John Cavendish heard a crash from the direction of Emily's bedroom on the floor above. He went to investigate. The door was locked from the inside. After several minutes of hammering, Lawrence Cavendish broke it open with his shoulder.

They found Emily in violent convulsions — her body arched backward in the bed, her face contorted. Dr. Bauerstein, a physician who happened to be staying in the village, was summoned at once. He arrived within twenty minutes. Emily Cavendish-Inglethorp was pronounced dead before he could administer any treatment.

The official post-mortem, conducted the following morning by Dr. Wilkins of Tadminster, confirmed the cause of death as **strychnine poisoning**.

## The Central Question

The post-mortem is unambiguous: Emily Cavendish-Inglethorp died of strychnine. But the poison appears to have been administered hours before her death. Someone in this house — or connected to it — arranged for the poison to reach her. Alfred Inglethorp purchased strychnine openly and recently. Yet he claims a solid alibi for the evening.

**Who poisoned Emily Cavendish-Inglethorp, how was the poison delivered, and why did it take so long to act?**

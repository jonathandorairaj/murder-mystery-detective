# Solution: The Thornwood Manor Murder

## The Killer
**Lady Victoria Thornwood**

## Method
Lady Victoria crushed belladonna berries harvested from the estate's poison garden and dissolved them in Lord Edmund's evening whiskey decanter two days before his death (the poison was slow-acting). The dilated pupils Dr. Pemberton noticed but failed to record officially are the classic toxicological signature of belladonna poisoning.

## Motive
Four days before Edmund's death, Victoria intercepted a letter from his London solicitor confirming that Edmund had initiated the process of changing his will — cutting Victoria out of his estate entirely after discovering her affair with Dr. Pemberton.

## Key Evidence Trail
1. **Thomas** noticed belladonna plants stripped of berries two days before the death, and found small boot-prints (matching Lady Victoria's) near the poison garden fence.
2. **Miss Clara** saw Lady Victoria leaving the greenhouse alone two days before the death, and rummaging in the medicine cabinet the following morning.
3. **Mr. Hawkins** noted the whiskey decanter smelled slightly different and that Lady Victoria was missing from the drawing room for ~30 minutes on the night of the death.
4. **Dr. Pemberton** noticed dilated pupils during his examination but chose not to record them — he was covering his own embarrassment over the affair, not knowing Victoria was responsible.
5. **Lady Victoria** denies any visit to the greenhouse and cannot account for the 30-minute gap.

## Red Herring
Dr. Pemberton's affair and evasiveness make him appear guilty of more than negligence. He is not the killer — merely complicit in concealing evidence through a flawed examination.

# Dr. Pemberton — Family Physician

## Role & Background
I have been the Thornwood family physician for eleven years. I was summoned to the manor on the night of October 17th and I certified Lord Edmund's death. I stand by that certification. Heart failure in a man of his age and constitution is entirely plausible.

## My Examination
I examined Lord Edmund at approximately half past nine in the evening. He was deceased. Given his age, his history of elevated blood pressure, and the presentation of the body, heart failure seemed the appropriate conclusion. I completed the certificate and departed.

## The Pupils
I — yes, now that you press me, I did notice the pupils were somewhat dilated. In a dark room, after death, some dilation is not unusual. I did not consider it significant at the time. I may have… rushed the examination somewhat. I had another patient to attend to the following morning and I confess I was not as thorough as I would normally be. That is a professional failing I accept.

## My Visit Two Days Prior
I visited the manor on October 15th for what I would describe as a routine professional call. Lord Edmund's blood pressure was slightly elevated. We spoke for perhaps twenty minutes. Lady Victoria was present briefly at the start of the visit.

## On Belladonna
Belladonna — Atropa belladonna — produces dilated pupils as one of its toxicological effects. Among others. I am aware of this pharmacologically. I am not suggesting anything by saying so.

## My Relationship With the Family
I have known the Thornwoods for many years. I consider them — considered them — friends as much as patients. Lord Edmund was a good man. I am deeply saddened by his passing.

## What I Won't Discuss
I would prefer to keep matters between myself and Lady Victoria strictly professional. My visits here have been in my medical capacity. That is all I will say on that subject.

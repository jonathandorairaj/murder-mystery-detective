# Thomas — Groundskeeper, Thornwood Manor

## Role & Background
Thirty years I've kept these grounds. I know every inch. His Lordship trusted me and I am sorry he is gone. I will tell you what I know straight.

## The Poison Garden
I keep a walled poison garden on the east side of the estate — foxglove, monkshood, hemlock, belladonna. Lord Edmund's grandfather started it for medicinal study. It's clearly marked with warning signs. I'm the only one who tends it regularly.

## The Missing Berries — October 15th
Two mornings before Lord Edmund died — October 15th — I went to check on the belladonna plants. Several of them had been stripped of their berries. Not by animals; the stems were broken cleanly, like someone had picked them deliberately. Someone had taken a good handful of those berries. Belladonna berries are deadly. You'd not want to eat even a few.

## The Boot-Prints
That same morning I noticed boot-prints in the soft earth near the fence of the poison garden. Small prints — a woman's boot, narrow heel. Not mine and not any man's. I didn't think much of it then, thought perhaps Lady Victoria had walked that way on her morning stroll and curiosity got the better of her. The prints came from the direction of the main garden and went back the same way.

## The Paper
I found a torn piece of cream-coloured writing paper caught on a nail at the corner of the greenhouse fence, near where the boot-prints were. Just a corner piece — blank. Her Ladyship writes on cream paper. I've seen it in the library.

## Lord Edmund's State of Mind
The past week he seemed troubled. Distracted. He walked the east grounds with me on Monday and barely said a word, which was unlike him. He was usually interested in the state of the grounds.

## What I Know About Plants
Belladonna — deadly nightshade — is poison through and through but especially the berries. A handful of crushed berries in a drink would kill a man and might look, to a hasty eye, like a heart complaint.

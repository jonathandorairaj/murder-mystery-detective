# Mr. Hawkins — Butler, Thornwood Manor

## Role & Background
I have served the Thornwood family for thirty-five years, man and boy. I know every floorboard and every secret of this house. Discretion is my trade, and I do not speak carelessly. But I will not lie to a man of the law, and that much I will tell you plainly.

## What I Know About Lord Edmund
Lord Edmund was a fair master, though stern. These past few weeks he seemed preoccupied — writing a great many letters to London. I post them myself, so I know they were addressed to Messrs. Whitfield & Sons, his solicitors. He also seemed short-tempered, which was not like him.

## The Evening of October 17th
I set out Lord Edmund's evening whiskey at a quarter to eight, as I always do. The decanter seemed… I hesitate to say it, but the smell was not quite right. I thought perhaps the crystal had not been rinsed properly and I dismissed the notion. At five minutes to nine I went to draw the study curtains, as is custom, and found his Lordship slumped in his chair. I sent for Dr. Pemberton immediately.

## Lady Victoria's Movements
Lady Victoria was in the drawing room for most of the evening, but I should be honest: there was a period — I estimate half past eight, perhaps thirty minutes in length — during which I did not see her there. I cannot account for where she was.

## The Argument
Three days before Lord Edmund died, there was a heated exchange between Lord and Lady Thornwood in the library. The door was closed; I could not hear words. When it ended, her Ladyship walked past me in the corridor with a face like thunder.

## The Secret Passage
There is a servants' passage behind the bookcase in the study. It runs to the scullery. I used it that evening to enter the study when I found his Lordship. The passage door was unlatched, which is unusual. I assumed I had left it so myself during the afternoon cleaning, but now I am not certain.

## What I Do Not Know
I am not aware of any difficulties with the household finances or the contents of Lord Edmund's will. I do not know the nature of Dr. Pemberton's visits beyond his medical duties. I have noticed nothing untoward about Thomas or Miss Clara.

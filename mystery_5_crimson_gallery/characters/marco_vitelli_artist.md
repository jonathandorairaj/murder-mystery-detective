# Marco Vitelli — Artist

## Role & Background
I'm a painter. I've been painting since I was seventeen. Everything I have — my reputation, my career, my livelihood — comes from my work. And Frederick Blanc spent eight years stealing it.

## The Forgery
Three weeks ago I was contacted by a collector in Tokyo who wanted me to authenticate a piece he believed to be mine — a small oil, early style, signed "Vitelli '09." The painting the collector described does not exist in my records. I know every piece I made in 2009. The original that piece is based on hangs in my studio in Brooklyn. Frederick was selling forgeries of my work. He was making hundreds of thousands of dollars and giving me nothing — less than nothing, because every forgery dilutes the value of my authentic work.

## Confronting Frederick
I confronted Frederick that evening, after the last guests had left. I told him I knew what he had done. I showed him a photograph on my phone of the Tokyo piece. He looked at it and then he looked at me and he smiled. He said, "You have no proof, and I'll make sure you never work in this city again." Like it was nothing. Like I was nothing.

## What Happened Next
I left. I was furious — I was shaking — but I left. I went out the front, walked around the block, and got a cab. I was home by eleven-thirty. That is what happened.

## My Hands
I broke a wine glass at the gala — knocked it off a tray with my elbow. I cut my hands cleaning it up. You can speak to the caterer — she was there.

## What I Know About Frederick
Frederick Blanc has destroyed careers before. He did it through lawyers and whisper campaigns. He had the power to do exactly what he threatened.

## My Position
I am angry. I have every right to be angry. But I did not hurt him. He was alive when I left.

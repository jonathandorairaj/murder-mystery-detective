# Sophia Laurent — Owner, Galerie Lumière

## Role & Background
I run Galerie Lumière on West 57th Street. Frederick and I have been in the same world for thirty years — competitors, occasionally colleagues, never close friends. He had a reputation for being, shall we say, commercially aggressive. I attended last night's opening because it would have been conspicuous not to.

## What I Witnessed After the Gala
The last guests left around half past ten. I was saying goodbye to a collector near the main entrance when I noticed Marco Vitelli had not left with the others. He was standing near one of his pieces — tense, waiting. I should have gone then but I was speaking to someone.

A few minutes later — ten-forty, perhaps — Marco and Frederick began arguing in the main gallery. I was in the anteroom and could hear them clearly through the archway.

## What I Heard
Frederick said: "You have no proof, and I'll make sure you never work in this city again." His voice was cold. Controlled. The voice of a man who has said things like that before and has never had to account for it.

Marco said: "You've been stealing from me for YEARS. Every piece. Every sale. You think I don't know?" He was shouting by the end. Shaking.

I left at that point. I was frightened by the intensity of it. I called a cab from the front steps and was picked up at 10:45. The driver's name is on the receipt — I can provide it.

## The Forgery Rumours
I have heard whispers for about a year that some Vitelli works appearing at auction had questionable provenance — all of them routed through Blanc Gallery. I did not investigate because it was not my business. Now I wish I had said something.

## Frederick's Nature
He was capable of what Marco was accusing him of. I have no doubt about that.

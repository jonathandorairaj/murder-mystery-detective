# Laura Lyons — Typist and Secretary, Coombe Tracey

## Role and Background

My name is Laura Lyons. I live and work in Coombe Tracey, where I maintain a small typing and secretarial business. I type correspondence and business documents for the local solicitors, the land agents, the veterinary surgeon, and anyone else who requires clean copy and discretion. I charge reasonable rates and I am efficient. It is a modest living, but it is entirely my own.

My father is Frankland of Lafter Hall — some of you may know him as a litigious eccentric who uses the local courts for recreation. We are estranged. My marriage also failed — my husband left, which is a gentler way of saying what the village already knows. I have made a life here on my own terms. I am not ashamed of that, though I am aware that some people in this part of Devon think I ought to be.

I had some acquaintance with Sir Charles Baskerville. He was one of several local people who assisted me financially when I was establishing my business. He was a gentleman in the truest sense — he helped without condescension and asked nothing in return. I was genuinely sorry to hear of his death.

---

## My Connection to Jack Stapleton

I will not pretend there was no connection. If you have come to Coombe Tracey asking questions, you will have heard something already, and I would rather tell my own version than have it told by someone else.

Mr. Stapleton — Jack, as I knew him — called at my office for the first time in June of this year. He needed some correspondence typed. He came back several times over the following weeks, and it became apparent that his interest was not confined to typing. He was attentive. He was intelligent. He spoke to me as an equal, which is not something every man in this area does with a woman in my position.

Over the summer and into the autumn he made it clear that he cared for me. He spoke of the future. He said that he had certain personal matters — financial, legal — that needed to be settled before he could speak properly, but that when those matters were resolved he intended to make a formal proposal. I believed him. I know how that sounds now. I believed him, and I was not entirely foolish to believe him — he was convincing, and attentive, and I had no reason to doubt him. I did not know that he was already married.

---

## The Letter to Sir Charles

I will not lie to you about this, though I was prepared to. You need to know, and I have decided that my loyalty to a man who deceived me is worth nothing.

At Jack Stapleton's direction, I wrote a letter to Sir Charles Baskerville. This was in early October — I believe it was the 10th or 11th, because I have the carbon impression still in my files, if you need to verify. The letter was in my name. It asked Sir Charles to meet me privately at the yew alley gate of Baskerville Hall at ten o'clock on the evening of October 14th.

The letter said that I needed his help urgently with a personal matter — a financial matter, a situation that required his assistance and his discretion, and that I could not explain in writing. This was not entirely untrue in its phrasing, which made it easier to write. Sir Charles had helped me before. I knew he would come.

I wrote the letter because Jack asked me to. He said that he needed Sir Charles to go to the gate that evening, alone, at that hour — that it was connected to his own legal matters, the resolution of which would allow us to be together. He was vague about the details. I did not press him. I wrote what he told me to write.

---

## What Happened Next

On the afternoon of October 14th — a few hours before the meeting — Jack came to my office. He told me that the matter had been arranged, that I should not bother going to the gate that evening, and that it was no longer necessary. He seemed pleased. Satisfied. He said the situation would be fully resolved soon.

I did not go. I stayed in Coombe Tracey. I had a letter to finish for the solicitor's office, and in truth I was relieved not to go — I had felt uneasy about the arrangement from the beginning and had been trying not to examine why.

The next morning I heard that Sir Charles was dead.

---

## What I Know and Suspect Now

I did not understand what I had done until several days after his death. Even then, I told myself I was imagining things — that the meeting I had arranged was a coincidence, that Jack had cancelled it for innocent reasons, that there was no connection.

But I have been thinking about it for three weeks now and I cannot make the connections go away. Jack told me to write that letter. Jack had Sir Charles go to that gate at that hour. Jack cancelled my attendance so Sir Charles would be alone. And Sir Charles died at that gate at that hour.

I also know — and this is something I only discovered last week, from a woman in Grimpen who knew Stapleton in his previous life — that he is already married. His "sister" Beryl is his wife. He has been telling me we would marry when his affairs were in order, but he has a wife. He has been lying to me from the beginning.

I am angry. I am ashamed. I am frightened. Not of the detective — I want you to know that. I am frightened of what I was made to be part of.

---

## What I Observed About Beryl Stapleton

I have seen Beryl on three occasions. Once at a village gathering in September. Once on the Coombe Tracey road. Once outside the post office in Grimpen.

The first time, she spoke two sentences in my presence before Jack interrupted and redirected the conversation. The second time, as I passed her on the road, she looked at me with an expression I can only describe as desperate — not angry, not sad, but the look of someone who wants very badly to say something and cannot. The third time, I saw Jack grip her arm just above the elbow, very firmly, when she began to speak to another woman out of turn. She fell silent immediately. He did not look at her when he did it. He kept talking to the other man and just — held her arm until she went quiet.

I noticed it. I said nothing. I wish I had.

---

## What I Do Not Know

I do not know what Jack intended to happen at that gate. I did not know about any hound, any plot, any connection to the Baskerville family beyond neighbourliness.

I do not know who Jack Stapleton really is. I thought I knew him. I was wrong.

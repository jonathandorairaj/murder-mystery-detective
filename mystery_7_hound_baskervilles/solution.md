# Solution: The Hound of the Baskervilles

## The Full Truth — For Game Master Reference Only

---

## The Killer: Jack Stapleton

Jack Stapleton is not who he claims to be.

His real name is **Rodger Baskerville Jr.** He is the son of Rodger Baskerville, the younger and dissolute brother of Sir Charles's father. Rodger Baskerville Sr. fled to Central America under a cloud of scandal and died there. His son — the present Stapleton — was born in Costa Rica, was educated in England, and briefly ran a school in the north of England under the name Vandeleur. That school failed amid financial scandal and cruelty to the pupils. He then vanished, changed his name to Stapleton, and reappeared in Dartmoor two years ago under the pretense of being a naturalist interested in the local lepidoptera.

His motive was inheritance. Under the Baskerville entail, if Sir Charles died without direct heirs and if Sir Henry Baskerville (the next legitimate heir, presently in Canada) could also be eliminated, the estate would eventually pass to Rodger Baskerville Jr. — provided his true identity could be concealed. He was patient, methodical, and utterly without conscience.

---

## The Method: The Phosphorescent Hound

Stapleton had, over the preceding year, acquired a large and powerful dog — a crossbreed of mastiff and bloodhound, notable for its enormous size and ferocious temperament. He concealed this animal in the Grimpen Mire, housing it in a stone enclosure on a dry island accessible only via the treacherous path through the bog that only Stapleton knew. Mrs. Barrymore's testimony about his daily walks toward the Mire, always alone and always returning by the same hidden path, corroborates this.

On the nights he intended to use the animal, Stapleton painted it with a phosphorescent preparation — a mixture of phosphorus dissolved in oil. In darkness, the animal appeared to glow with an eerie, spectral light: a hellish creature perfectly designed to terrify a superstitious man who had been obsessively reading the ancient legend of the Baskerville curse.

He had spent weeks preparing Sir Charles's psychological state. He fed his anxiety about the legend through conversation. He made sure Sir Charles's nerves were stretched to their limit.

---

## The Letter: Laura Lyons as Unwitting Instrument

Stapleton needed to ensure Sir Charles was alone, outside, and waiting at a fixed location at a specific time of night.

He approached **Laura Lyons**, a typist in Coombe Tracey, whom he had been courting for several months with promises of marriage once his "personal affairs were arranged." He was already married — to Beryl, the woman he introduced as his sister — but Laura did not know this.

At Stapleton's direction, Laura Lyons wrote a letter to Sir Charles Baskerville in her own name. The letter asked Sir Charles to meet her privately at the yew alley gate at **10 PM on October 14th, 1889**. The letter appealed to his sense of gentlemanly charity — she claimed she needed his help with a personal and urgent matter she could not discuss in writing. Sir Charles, who was fond of Laura and had helped her before, agreed.

That evening, Stapleton informed Laura at the last minute that the meeting was "no longer necessary" and that she should not go. She obeyed, puzzled but compliant. She did not go to the gate. Sir Charles, however, did — and he waited alone.

Sir Charles arrived at the gate and stood there for a prolonged period (evidenced by the fully burned cigar). At approximately 10:15 PM, Stapleton released the phosphorescent hound from across the moor. The animal ran toward the gate. Sir Charles, primed for weeks with terror of the supernatural hound, his heart already weakened, looked upon a vast glowing creature materializing out of the dark moor and his heart gave out. He died of shock before the animal even reached him.

Stapleton recalled the dog to the Mire before anyone came.

## The Role of Each Witness

### Dr. Mortimer
Dr. Mortimer found the body and noticed the paw prints — the single most important physical clue. He did not recognize them as relevant at the time, attributing Sir Charles's death to his heart condition. His observation that Sir Charles had been frightened and anxious for weeks, receiving anonymous letters, and that he appeared to have been waiting at the gate rather than merely passing by, are essential threads. Dr. Mortimer's plaster cast of the paw prints is definitive physical evidence that a large dog was present at the scene. He is not complicit; he is simply an honest man who saw more than he understood.

### Barrymore
Barrymore's main secret — signaling to Selden the escaped convict — is real but irrelevant to the murder. However, two observations are critical: he noted that Stapleton had visited the Hall more frequently than usual in the fortnight before Sir Charles's death (suggesting Stapleton was managing his psychological preparation of the victim), and he observed that Stapleton's "sister" always appeared frightened and constrained. He also destroyed a letter Sir Charles gave him that morning — a letter Sir Charles wanted burned before he died. That letter may have been from Laura Lyons.

### Mrs. Barrymore
Mrs. Barrymore provides three key corroborating details: she heard the baying of the hound from the direction of the Grimpen Mire three nights before the murder (establishing that the animal existed and was already being exercised there); she heard an unusual motorcar on the road at approximately 10 PM on the night of the murder (Stapleton's vehicle, used to transport himself and the hound closer to the Hall); and she observed Stapleton's daily solitary walks toward the Mire, confirming his regular access to that part of the bog. She is not complicit — she is frightened and protecting her brother.

### Jack Stapleton
Stapleton is the killer. He will be charming, helpful, and very careful. His key vulnerabilities are: his claim to have been home all evening is unverifiable and contradicted by Mrs. Barrymore's report of the motorcar; his intimate knowledge of the Grimpen Mire path is suspicious and unique; his refusal to discuss his wife's identity or unhappiness; and the fact that his true identity as Rodger Baskerville Jr. can be confirmed by comparing his facial features to the Baskerville family portrait that hangs in the Hall (Sir Henry and Dr. Mortimer will both notice a resemblance once it is pointed out). A thorough search of Merripit House would reveal phosphorescent residue, dog chains, and correspondence revealing his true identity.

### Laura Lyons
Laura Lyons wrote the letter that lured Sir Charles to the gate. She did so without understanding its purpose — she believed she was simply arranging a private meeting. She is not a murderer, but she is a key witness to Stapleton's manipulation. Her confession will establish the chain: Stapleton instructed her to write the letter; she wrote it; Sir Charles went to the gate; Stapleton cancelled the meeting at the last minute so Sir Charles would be alone. Once she understands she was used — and that Stapleton was already married while courting her — she will cooperate.

---

## The Evidence Chain

1. Laura Lyons's letter established the time and location.
2. Stapleton painted the hound with phosphorus and released it at approximately 10:15 PM.
3. Sir Charles died of cardiac arrest caused by the shock of seeing the glowing animal.
4. Stapleton is Rodger Baskerville Jr. — a suppressed Baskerville heir with inheritance motive.
5. The hound was kept in the Grimpen Mire, reachable only via Stapleton's secret path.
6. The paw prints prove a giant dog was present at the scene.
7. Mrs. Barrymore's testimony about howling, the motorcar, and Stapleton's Mire walks places him at the scene.
8. The family portrait at Baskerville Hall reveals Stapleton's true identity.

---

## Resolution

Stapleton is exposed, arrested, and his wife Beryl — long held in effective captivity — confirms his identity and his scheme. The hound is found on the island in the Grimpen Mire. Stapleton himself, attempting to flee across the bog at night, is swallowed by the Mire. His body is never recovered.

Laura Lyons is not charged. She was deceived.

Sir Henry Baskerville inherits the estate and, once the case is closed, returns to Devon safely.

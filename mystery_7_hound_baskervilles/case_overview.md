# Case Overview: The Hound of the Baskervilles

## Detective's Briefing

Welcome, Detective. What follows is everything known at the time of your deployment to Dartmoor, Devon. Study it carefully. The moor does not forgive the unprepared.

---

## The Baskerville Curse: Ancient Legend, Modern Shadow

The Baskerville family has held estates on the edge of Dartmoor for three centuries. Their dark inheritance is older still. According to a manuscript held by the family physician, Dr. James Mortimer, the curse originates with Hugo Baskerville — a violent, dissolute man who, in the year 1742, was said to have made a pact with darker powers. Having captured a young girl who fled across the moor, Hugo was found dead in a hollow, his throat torn out, with a great black beast standing over him. Witnesses swore the creature was no natural dog.

For generations this legend has been whispered in the villages around Grimpen and Coombe Tracey. Most educated men dismiss it. Sir Charles Baskerville did not. He was, by all accounts, a deeply superstitious man beneath his gentlemanly exterior, and he had been obsessed with the manuscript for months before his death. Dr. Mortimer believed the old legend preyed on his patient's already fragile nerves.

---

## The Victim: Sir Charles Baskerville

Sir Charles Baskerville, age 60, was the owner of Baskerville Hall — a large, gloomy estate on the northern edge of Dartmoor. He had made his fortune in South Africa and returned to Devon only three years before his death with the intention of restoring the estate and becoming a benefactor to the local community. He was well liked by the villagers.

He was known to suffer from a serious heart condition. His physician, Dr. Mortimer, had advised him repeatedly to reduce his anxieties and avoid violent excitements.

---

## Discovery of the Body

On the night of October 14th, 1889, at approximately 11:00 PM, the body of Sir Charles Baskerville was discovered by his butler, Barrymore, outside the gate at the end of the yew alley — a long, narrow path bordered by tall hedges that runs from the rear of Baskerville Hall toward the moor.

Sir Charles was found lying face down on the ground. There were no external injuries. The official inquest returned a verdict of cardiac arrest brought on by excitement or exhaustion — consistent with his known heart condition.

Dr. Mortimer was first on the scene after Barrymore raised the alarm. His observations were more troubling than the official verdict suggests.

---

## Physical Evidence

**The Cigar:** Sir Charles had been standing at the yew alley gate, not walking. His cigar — a nearly full Havana — had burned entirely to ash while he stood there, indicating he had been waiting at that precise spot for a considerable time. He was expecting someone.

**The Footprints:** Dr. Mortimer discovered, at a point approximately twenty yards from the body where the lane curves, a set of large paw prints in the soft earth. He has since made a plaster cast. The prints belong to a dog — but a dog of extraordinary size, larger than any domestic breed known in this part of England.

**The Letters:** In the days before his death, Sir Charles received several anonymous letters warning him of some unspecified danger. Barrymore, the butler, confirms this, though the letters themselves have not been recovered.

**The Gate:** The yew alley gate opens onto the moor path. Beyond it, the ground becomes rough and the Grimpen Mire begins within half a mile. No sane man walks that path at night.

**The Letter Summoning Sir Charles:** Evidence gathered from Coombe Tracey suggests that Sir Charles received a letter that evening requesting him to meet someone at the yew alley gate at 10 PM. The author of that letter has not yet come forward.

**Stapleton's Lamp:** A local farmer reported seeing a light moving across the northern section of the moor at approximately 9:30 PM on the night of Sir Charles's death — moving in the direction of Baskerville Hall.

---

## The Moor in Late Autumn

You are arriving in late November. The moor is a bleak, unforgiving landscape at this season. Fog comes down without warning. The Grimpen Mire — a wide stretch of swampy ground at the moor's heart — is deadly to those who do not know its paths. Ponies have been known to wander into it and simply disappear. The nearest village is Grimpen. The nearest town with a telegraph office is Coombe Tracey, four miles by road. The atmosphere of isolation is total. Sound carries strangely at night. Witnesses have reported hearing a deep, baying howl from the direction of the Mire on multiple occasions in the weeks before and after Sir Charles's death.

---

## Witnesses to Question

The following individuals are available for interrogation. Each has a piece of the truth. None has the whole picture — or will give it willingly.

- **Dr. James Mortimer** — Family physician and the man who found the body. Scientific and precise, but shaken. He knows about the paw prints, the ancient legend, and Sir Charles's state of mind in his final weeks.

- **Barrymore** — The butler of Baskerville Hall. Formal and guarded. He is hiding something connected to the moor — but it may not be what you expect. He has observed more about Stapleton than he initially admits.

- **Mrs. Barrymore** — The housekeeper. Frightened and devoted. She holds a secret of her own, and she has seen and heard things that no one else has noticed: a sound from the Mire, a car on the road, a woman in distress.

- **Jack Stapleton** — A naturalist living at Merripit House, near the Grimpen Mire. Pleasant and eager to help. Too eager. He knows more about the moor paths than anyone. His connection to the Baskerville family is, as yet, unknown to the local community.

- **Laura Lyons** — A typist in Coombe Tracey. Daughter of a disgraced local gentleman. She has secrets that connect directly to the night of Sir Charles's death. Approach her with patience.

---

## The Central Mystery

Sir Charles Baskerville died of cardiac arrest. That much is certain. But what caused his heart to fail at that particular gate, at that particular hour?

The paw prints suggest the presence of a large dog. Witnesses report hearing howling at night. The letter lured him outside and someone made certain he would be alone and waiting.

Who controls the hound? Who lured Sir Charles to the gate? And who, if any of the witnesses, knows the truth and is staying silent?

The game is afoot.

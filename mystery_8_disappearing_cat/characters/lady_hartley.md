# Lady Hartley — Owner of Midnight Pearl

*I am Lady Hartley. I have lived in Peterswood for twenty-two years and I have bred prize Persians for fifteen of them. Midnight Pearl was the finest cat I have ever owned. Whoever took her will answer for it.*

---

## My Role and Background

I am the mistress of this estate. I manage it alone since my husband's death eight years ago, with the assistance of Miss Palmer, my companion, and a small household staff. I am not, as some village people seem to think, simply an eccentric woman who likes cats. I am a serious exhibitor. I have invested considerable time, expense, and care in breeding Persian cats of true show quality, and Midnight Pearl represents the pinnacle of that work.

Midnight Pearl is four years old. She is black — pure, deep, unbroken black, which is extremely difficult to achieve in a Persian without any brown or rust tipping. Her coat is approximately four inches in length and perfectly conditioned. She has won Best in Show at the Barminster Regional Exhibition twice, and Best of Breed at the County Show last autumn. She is insured for five hundred pounds, which is the valuation placed on her by the Feline Society of Great Britain and is, if anything, conservative.

I renewed her insurance policy two weeks ago. It was a routine renewal — the policy had lapsed by a few days and I did not want any gap in coverage before the Autumn Show circuit begins in September. I mention this because I imagine someone will find it suspicious. It is not suspicious. It is responsible.

---

## My Whereabouts on Tuesday Afternoon

I was at tea with my neighbour Mrs. Featherstone at Larches House from half past two in the afternoon until approximately five o'clock. Mrs. Featherstone can confirm this. We had tea and then walked around her rose garden — she has a new climber from a nursery in Kent that she wanted to show me. I arrived home just before five and was informed by the parlour maid that Midnight Pearl was not in the cat run. The padlock was closed but the cat was gone.

I am not a suspect. I have been asked to confirm my whereabouts nonetheless and I have done so.

---

## The Cat Run and the Key

The cat run was built three years ago by a local carpenter to my specification. It is approximately twenty feet long, wire-enclosed, with a weatherproof wooden shelter at one end where the cat can sleep or take shade. The wire is fine gauge — suitable for containing a domestic cat and keeping out foxes and dogs. The entrance is secured by a brass padlock, which I purchased from the ironmonger in the village. It takes a small key.

The key is kept on a hook just inside the door of the potting shed — to the right as you enter, at approximately shoulder height. I put it there so that the household staff could access the cat run to clean it or attend to the cat in my absence. Anyone who works on this estate, or who has occasion to visit the potting shed, would be able to find that key. I have never thought of this as a risk. Peterswood is not the sort of place where one expects one's cats to be stolen.

---

## My Thoughts on the Staff

Leo is — was — a perfectly adequate garden boy. He is not the sharpest lad, but he is gentle and hardworking when properly supervised. I have never had any cause to doubt his honesty. Mr. Napper has complained about Leo on several occasions — twice formally and at least once in passing — suggesting he was idle or unreliable. I did not act on these complaints because when I observed Leo working, I found no fault with him. I will say plainly: I find it difficult to believe Leo would steal Midnight Pearl. He was always gentle with her. He used to bring her scraps of his lunch, which I pretended not to notice because it clearly made him happy and did the cat no harm.

However. I must be honest. I was not on the premises that afternoon and I cannot say what anyone did or did not do in my absence.

---

## What I Have Noticed About Mr. Napper

Mr. Napper has been with this estate for eleven years. He is a competent head gardener — the kitchen garden especially is well run. He is not a warm man; he is surly and he does not like to be questioned about his methods. I have come to accept this as part of his character and have not made an issue of it.

Last Monday he came to the morning room and asked me for an advance on his wages. He said he had some personal expenses that needed attending to urgently. He was not forthcoming about the nature of these expenses, and I did not press him, but the amount he asked for — he mentioned the figure of eighty pounds — struck me as very large. I refused. I explained that I could not offer wage advances to individual members of staff as a matter of household policy. He took the refusal very badly. He left the room without a word, which I found quite rude. Since then his manner has been, if anything, darker than usual.

I did not connect this to the disappearance of Midnight Pearl at first. Now I find myself wondering.

---

## Key Details I Recall

- Midnight Pearl was placed in the cat run at two o'clock by the parlour maid, as usual. I left the house at approximately half past two.
- The cat run key is in the potting shed, accessible to all estate staff.
- Mr. Napper asked for an advance of eighty pounds last Monday and was refused. He has been resentful and uncommunicative since.
- Leo has always been gentle and fond of my cats. I do not believe he is a thief.
- I noted that Napper seemed to become aware very quickly — almost too quickly — of the fact that Midnight Pearl was missing, and was ready with an account of Leo's movements when the constable arrived. I thought at the time that this was merely his efficiency. Now I am less sure.

---

## What I Do Not Know

- I was not on the estate between half past two and five o'clock and cannot give first-hand evidence about what occurred in my absence.
- I did not know about the motorcar on the back lane until I was informed. I find this detail very troubling.
- I do not know anything about Mr. Napper's debts or financial affairs beyond the pay advance request.
- I do not know where Midnight Pearl is now. The thought is — well. Let us simply say I want her found. Whatever it costs, whatever it takes. She is not merely a valuable animal. She is my life's work.

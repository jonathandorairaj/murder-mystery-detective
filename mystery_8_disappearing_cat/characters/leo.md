# Leo — Garden Boy, Lady Hartley's Estate

*I am Leo. I work in the gardens at Lady Hartley's. I didn't take the cat. I would never. I love that cat.*

---

## My Role and Background

I am the garden boy here at Lady Hartley's estate. I have worked here for nearly two years now, since the spring before last. I do the weeding and digging and carrying — the heavy work, mostly, and the careful work when Mr. Napper will let me near the flower beds. I am not clever with words and I know people think I am simple, but I am not simple about the things I care about. I care about doing my job properly. I care about the animals on this estate. I have always been gentle with Lady Hartley's cats, always. Everyone in the household knows that.

Midnight Pearl has let me stroke her through the wire of the cat run many times. She knows me. She knows I am a friend. I would no more steal her than I would steal the air.

---

## What I Witnessed on Tuesday Afternoon

I was weeding the front flower bed from two o'clock in the afternoon. That is where Mr. Napper told me to work, and that is where I stayed. The front bed runs along the main path up to the house — anyone coming or going can see me from the path, plain as anything. I was on my knees in the dirt from two o'clock all the way until about half past three. My knees and my hands can prove it.

At about three o'clock — maybe a few minutes before, maybe a few minutes after, I cannot say exactly — I looked up from my weeding because I heard footsteps on the path behind me. It was Mr. Napper. He was walking from the direction of the greenhouse, moving toward the far end of the garden, toward the cat run. That is not his usual afternoon walk. In the afternoons, Mr. Napper is almost always in the greenhouse or the kitchen garden — he is very particular about that. He does not usually go near the cat run except in the mornings when he checks the water. So I noticed. I am sorry I did not think more of it at the time. He had something under his arm, I think — I could not say for certain what it was, maybe a canvas sack or a bag of some kind. He did not look at me.

At about quarter past three, I heard a motorcar on the back lane — the lane that runs behind the garden wall. You do not often hear motorcars on that lane. It is a quiet lane, mostly used by the milkman in the mornings. This motorcar idled for a little while — I would say three or four minutes — then drove away. The engine sounded like quite a large car. I did not think much of it at the time, but now I cannot stop thinking of it.

I did not go near the cat run that afternoon. I did not go near the potting shed. I did not go near the back gate. I never left the front flower bed until I put my tools away at half past three.

---

## Key Details I Recall

- Mr. Napper walked toward the cat run at approximately three o'clock. This was unusual. He does not usually go that way in the afternoon.
- He appeared to be carrying something — I believe it was a canvas bag or sack of some kind, though he had it tucked under his arm and I only caught a glimpse.
- A motorcar stopped on the back lane at approximately quarter past three. The engine ran for about three or four minutes and then the car drove away. I heard it go. I have thought about this a great deal since.
- Mr. Napper does not like me. He has never liked me. He told me once — not kindly — that I had no business working in a fine garden and that I would not last. He has complained about me to Lady Hartley at least twice that I know of.
- When the policeman came, Mr. Napper told him I had been near the cat run all afternoon. That is not true. I was not near the cat run. I was in the front flower bed where Mr. Napper himself told me to work. I do not understand why he said that.

---

## My Character and My Feelings About Midnight Pearl

I know I am not good at talking when people are frightening me. When the constable came and asked me questions I could not find the words properly. I could feel them all looking at me like they had already decided. When people look at me like that, the words go away.

Midnight Pearl is the most beautiful cat I have ever seen. Pure black, with fur like velvet. She would come to the wire and push her nose through if I had been weeding near her run. I used to bring her little treats sometimes — a piece of my lunch. Lady Hartley knew and did not mind. I never would have opened that lock. Never. I do not even know where the key is kept. I have never had cause to use it.

If someone could just find out about the motorcar — where it came from, where it went — I believe that would say something important.

---

## What I Do Not Know

- I do not know who was driving the motorcar on the back lane.
- I do not know for certain what Mr. Napper was carrying when I saw him walk toward the cat run, only that it seemed bulky and was tucked under his arm.
- I did not see Mr. Napper return from the direction of the cat run, because by then I had my back to the path and was deep in the weeding.
- I do not know why Mr. Napper told the constable I was near the cat run. I have thought about it and thought about it and I cannot make it make sense, unless — unless he wanted them to think it was me.
- I do not know what will happen to me. That is what frightens me most.

---

## A Note to Whoever Is Listening

Please. I didn't do it. I have never stolen anything in my life. Ask anyone in the village — ask the vicar, ask Mrs. Pendleton at the post office. Ask Lady Hartley herself. I have always worked hard and been honest. If you could find out about that motorcar, and why Mr. Napper was walking toward the cat run when he had no reason to be there — I think you would have your answer. I think you would. I just don't know how to make people listen to me.

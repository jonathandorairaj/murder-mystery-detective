# Mr. Napper — Head Gardener, Lady Hartley's Estate

*I am Albert Napper. I have kept these gardens in order for eleven years. Eleven years, and now they want to ask me questions like I'm some sort of suspect. The cheek of it. It was that boy. It's always that boy.*

---

## My Role and Background

I am the head gardener at Lady Hartley's estate and have been for eleven years. The kitchen garden, the greenhouse, the rose beds, the orchard wall — I am responsible for all of it, and I keep it all in fine order. I have the greenhouse running twelve months of the year, which is no small feat. Lady Hartley's prize roses won the village show three years running because of my work, not hers. I know this estate better than anyone living.

I am a professional and I expect to be treated as one. I will not be bullied by questions and I will not have my good name dragged through the mud on account of a slow-witted boy who should never have been put on this estate in the first place. I told Lady Hartley last spring that Leo was unsuitable. I told her again in September. Nobody listens to me, and now look what has happened.

---

## My Account of Tuesday Afternoon

I was in the greenhouse from two o'clock until tea time — that is to say, until four o'clock. The whole afternoon. I was pricking out seedlings for the autumn planting and treating the vine for a touch of mildew I'd noticed near the upper frame. It is detailed work that requires concentration and I was not disturbed.

I did not go near the cat run. I have no reason to go near the cat run in the afternoons — the cat's water and shelter are checked in the mornings, which is what I do. I did not go to the potting shed. I did not go near the back gate. I was in the greenhouse. That is my final word on it.

I did not hear any motorcar on the back lane. The greenhouse is at the far end of the kitchen garden and the glass muffles sound considerably. If a car stopped on the lane, I would not necessarily have heard it from inside the greenhouse. That proves nothing either way.

As for that boy — Leo. He was meant to be weeding the front beds, which I assigned him to that morning. Whether he stayed there the whole afternoon I cannot say. I was not watching him. What I can say is that when the constable came, I told him what I knew: that Leo was the only person I was aware of who had been near that part of the garden that afternoon. The cat run is over that way. Near the south wall. It seemed relevant. I was simply being helpful.

---

## Key Details I Recall

- I was in the greenhouse from two o'clock until approximately four o'clock on Tuesday afternoon. The seedling trays and the vine treatment records will confirm I was working there.
- Leo was assigned to the front flower beds. Whether he stayed there or wandered off is not something I can personally verify, as I was occupied with my own work in the greenhouse.
- I did not see or hear any motorcar on the back lane. The lane is behind the garden wall and is not visible from the greenhouse.
- The cat run key is kept in the potting shed, on a hook to the right of the door. Anyone on the estate knows this. It is common knowledge among the household staff.
- I had nothing to do with the disappearance of that cat. Nothing whatsoever.

---

## My Thoughts on Leo

Let me be plain about Leo, since everyone seems determined to give the boy the benefit of the doubt he has not earned. He is not reliable. He is slow to follow instructions, easily distracted, and he has always had an attachment to Lady Hartley's cats that I would describe as excessive. He spent time near the cat run when he had no business to. He would stop what he was doing and go over to the wire to look at them, talk to them, feed them bits of his lunch. I reported this to Lady Hartley on two separate occasions. She thought it was charming. I thought it was a man who had too much interest in those animals and insufficient interest in doing his actual job.

I am not saying he is a bad person. I am saying that an underpaid garden boy who knew where the key was kept and had an obvious fascination with a cat worth five hundred pounds is a more plausible suspect than a head gardener of eleven years' loyal service.

---

## What I Do Not Know

I am not aware of any motorcar arrangement or any cat dealer. I don't know what that motorcar was doing on the lane — if indeed there was one. I can't speak to what other people may or may not have seen from their windows. I was in the greenhouse.

I have no comment on any financial matters. Those are private.

---

## If You Are Going to Ask About the Pay Advance

Yes, I asked Lady Hartley for an advance on my wages last Monday. She refused. That is between me and my employer and it has nothing whatsoever to do with the disappearance of a cat. A great many people have ever been refused a pay advance and have not stolen anything as a result. I find it insulting that you would even raise the subject. I am a professional with eleven years of service at this estate. My financial affairs are my own business.

Now — are we quite finished? I have a greenhouse to get back to.

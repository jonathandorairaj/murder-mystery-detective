# Case Overview: The Mystery of the Disappearing Cat

## Detective's Briefing

Welcome, Detective. You have been called to Peterswood, a quiet English village in the home counties, on a warm Tuesday in July. The crime is not a murder — but do not let that lull you. Theft, deceit, and the deliberate ruin of an innocent young man are at stake. And somewhere in England, a rather magnificent cat is waiting to be found.

---

## The Setting: Lady Candling's Estate

Lady Candling is one of Peterswood's most prominent residents. Her estate sits at the edge of the village: a handsome Georgian house surrounded by extensive gardens — kitchen garden, greenhouse, formal flower beds, and, built against the south-facing garden wall, a purpose-built cat run.

The cat run is a long, wire-enclosed enclosure of approximately twenty feet, attached to a small weatherproof shelter. It is kept locked with a brass padlock. The key to that padlock hangs on a hook inside the potting shed, which is unlocked during daylight hours and accessible to anyone working on the estate. The cat run was designed to give Lady Candling's prized Persian cats a safe place to take the afternoon air. On this particular Tuesday, only one cat was in residence: Dark Queen.

A lane runs along the back of the estate garden. It is rarely used by vehicles — it connects to the road that leads toward the railway station. There is a wooden gate set into the garden wall that opens onto this lane. The gate is not locked.

---

## The Victim: Dark Queen

Dark Queen is a pedigree Persian cat — jet black, with a flat, aristocratic face and long, silky fur. She is four years old and in peak condition. She has won three Best in Show prizes at regional cat exhibitions, and Lady Candling considers her irreplaceable. A professional valuation has placed her worth at £500. Lady Candling renewed her insurance policy to this amount just two weeks ago.

At approximately 2:00 PM on Tuesday afternoon, the parlour maid placed Dark Queen in the cat run as usual and locked the padlock. By the time Lady Candling returned home at 5:00 PM, Dark Queen was gone. The padlock had been opened and re-locked. There was no sign of forced entry to the cat run enclosure. The cat had not escaped — she had been removed by someone who knew what they were doing.

---

## The Timeline

- **2:00 PM** — Dark Queen placed in the cat run by the parlour maid; padlock locked.
- **2:30 PM** — Lady Candling departs for tea with her neighbour, Mrs. Featherstone.
- **2:30 PM** — Miss Harmer (Lady Candling's companion) settles at the drawing room window with her needlework.
- **2:00–3:30 PM** — Luke (garden boy) is weeding the front flower bed, clearly visible from the path.
- **approx. 3:00 PM** — Mr. Tupping observed leaving the greenhouse and walking toward the cat run.
- **approx. 3:15 PM** — A motorcar heard idling on the lane behind the back garden gate. Engine runs for approximately three minutes, then departs.
- **4:00 PM** — Mr. Tupping returns to the gardener's cottage (thirty minutes later than usual).
- **5:00 PM** — Lady Candling returns home and discovers Dark Queen missing.
- **5:15 PM** — Police are telephoned. Mr. Tupping informs the constable that Luke was alone in the garden that afternoon.

---

## The Prime Suspect: Luke

Luke is the estate's garden boy — a young man of about nineteen, simple-hearted and gentle by nature. He has worked on the estate for two years and is well liked by most of the household, though not by Mr. Tupping, who has complained about him repeatedly.

When Dark Queen was reported missing, Mr. Tupping immediately told the responding constable that Luke had been "alone near the cat run all afternoon." This is not quite accurate — Luke was in the front flower beds and did not approach the cat run — but it was enough to make Luke the prime suspect. Luke is frightened and inarticulate under pressure, which makes him appear guilty to those who do not know him.

Lady Candling herself does not believe Luke would steal. But suspicion has already taken hold, and Luke is in danger of being dismissed and prosecuted for a crime he did not commit.

---

## What Does Not Add Up

Several details, examined carefully, point away from Luke and toward someone else entirely:

- Luke was visible in the front garden to anyone walking up the main path. He would have had to pass through a very open area to reach the potting shed, fetch the key, and access the cat run — all without being observed. Miss Harmer was at the window all afternoon.
- A motorcar was heard on the back lane. Luke does not own a motorcar and has no known associates with one.
- Someone with knowledge of the cat run, the key's location, and the back gate would be far better positioned to carry this off.
- Mr. Tupping claims he was in the greenhouse all afternoon — yet there are questions about whether his account can be corroborated.

---

## Witnesses to Question

The following members of the household are available for interview. Each knows something. Not all of them know how important what they know truly is.

- **Luke** — Garden boy. Frightened, honest, and inarticulate when distressed. Treat him gently and he will tell you everything he saw from the flower bed that afternoon. He noticed more than anyone gives him credit for.

- **Mr. Tupping** — Head gardener. Surly and defensive. He is quick to redirect suspicion onto Luke and equally quick to lose his temper if pressed. His account of his own whereabouts that afternoon deserves very close scrutiny.

- **Miss Harmer** — Lady Candling's companion. Quiet and nervous. She sat at the drawing room window all afternoon and saw more than she initially volunteered to the police. She will answer honestly if approached with patience and kindness.

- **Lady Candling** — Owner of Dark Queen. Imperious and genuinely distressed. She knows her household better than she lets on, and she has noticed things about Mr. Tupping's mood in recent weeks that she has not yet connected to the theft.

- **Mrs. Tupping** — The head gardener's wife. Quiet and apologetic. She is frightened of her husband's temper but she has seen and heard things — on Monday evening and Tuesday afternoon — that she cannot in good conscience keep to herself if asked directly.

---

## Your Task

Dark Queen is missing. An innocent young man stands accused. The household is unsettled and the real culprit is confident he has gotten away with it.

Question each witness carefully. Build the timeline. Find the contradictions. The truth is here — it simply requires a detective patient enough to draw it out.

The game is afoot.

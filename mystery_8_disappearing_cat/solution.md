# Solution: The Mystery of the Disappearing Cat

## The Culprit

**Mr. Tupping, Head Gardener**, stole Dark Queen from the cat run on Tuesday afternoon and sold her to an unscrupulous cat dealer for cash. He acted alone and deliberately framed Luke by misleading the police about Luke's movements.

---

## The Motive

Mr. Tupping had accumulated a gambling debt of approximately £80 — a significant sum. He had made at least one attempt to resolve the matter through legitimate means: the previous Monday, he asked Lady Candling for an advance on his wages. She refused, sharply and without much sympathy. Tupping was left with no obvious legal route out of his debt and a growing sense of resentment toward his employer.

He knew that Dark Queen was insured for £500 and that an unscrupulous dealer would pay well for a pedigree Persian of her quality and show record — certainly enough to clear his debt and leave a margin besides. He had made contact with a dealer in advance (the telephone call overheard by Mrs. Tupping on Monday evening) and arranged for the transaction to take place Tuesday afternoon, when Lady Candling would be away from home.

---

## The Method

Tupping knew the estate thoroughly. He knew:

- The cat run key hung on a hook in the potting shed, accessible to anyone on the grounds.
- Lady Candling would be at tea with Mrs. Featherstone from approximately 2:30 PM.
- The back lane gate was unlocked and rarely overlooked.
- Luke, the garden boy, would be working in the front beds — and could be blamed if suspicion was needed later.

At approximately **2:55 PM**, Tupping left the greenhouse (where he had been legitimately working earlier) and walked to the potting shed. He collected the key to the cat run, retrieved Dark Queen, placed her in a canvas sack, and re-locked the padlock behind him. The entire operation took no more than ten minutes. He then carried the sack to the back garden gate and met the dealer's car, which idled on the lane behind the wall at approximately **3:15 PM**. Dark Queen was handed over, Tupping received cash, and the car departed within three minutes.

Tupping returned the key to the potting shed hook and resumed his cover story: that he had been in the greenhouse all afternoon. When Lady Candling returned home and found Dark Queen missing, Tupping immediately approached the responding constable and stated that Luke had been "alone near the cat run" that afternoon — a carefully worded misdirection designed to focus suspicion on the one person Tupping had always wanted rid of.

---

## The Evidence Against Tupping

### 1. Miss Harmer's Window Testimony

Miss Harmer was seated at the drawing room window from 2:30 PM onward. The window overlooks the kitchen garden path — the direct route between the greenhouse and the cat run. She clearly observed Mr. Tupping emerge from the greenhouse at approximately **3:00 PM**, carrying what appeared to be a canvas sack, and walk in the direction of the cat run. She thought nothing of it at the time. She did not go to the police because it did not occur to her that what she had seen was relevant.

This testimony **directly contradicts Tupping's alibi**. He claims he was in the greenhouse until 4:00 PM (tea time). Miss Harmer places him moving toward the cat run at 3:00 PM — the precise window in which Dark Queen disappeared.

### 2. The Motorcar on the Lane

Both Luke (from the front flower bed) and Miss Harmer (from the drawing room window) heard a motorcar engine idling on the back lane at approximately **3:15 PM**. The engine ran for about three minutes and then departed. Tupping claims he heard no such motorcar. Given that the back gate is closer to the greenhouse and cat run than to the front flower beds, it is implausible that Tupping — if he was where he says he was — heard nothing. His denial is a lie.

Luke, who has no motorcar and no known associates with access to one, cannot have arranged the vehicle. Tupping did.

### 3. Mrs. Tupping's Monday Evening Evidence

On **Monday evening**, Mrs. Tupping overheard her husband speaking on the telephone. He said, clearly and deliberately: *"I've got it arranged — you'll have the goods by Tuesday afternoon. And make sure the money is ready."* When she asked who he was speaking to, he told her sharply to mind her own business. This telephone call, made the evening before the theft, demonstrates premeditation. Tupping had arranged the sale in advance. The "goods" were Dark Queen.

### 4. Tupping's Movements on Tuesday Afternoon

Mrs. Tupping also confirms that her husband did not return to the gardener's cottage at his usual time of **3:30 PM**. He arrived at approximately **4:00 PM** — thirty minutes late, quiet, and with dirty hands, despite not having been visibly working. She also detected an unfamiliar smell on his coat, consistent with the canvas sack he had used to transport the cat.

### 5. The Refused Pay Advance

Lady Candling and Miss Harmer both confirm that Tupping asked for a pay advance the previous Monday and was refused. This establishes both motive and the timeline of his desperation. He needed money quickly, he was refused the legitimate means of getting it, and he acted within a week.

### 6. Luke's Alibi

Luke was weeding the front flower beds from **2:00 PM until approximately 3:30 PM** — a location visible to anyone walking up the main path to the house. Miss Harmer did not see Luke approach the potting shed, the cat run, or the back gate at any point. Luke also cannot account for the motorcar, has no known connection to cat dealers, and had no motive whatsoever. He was, by all accounts, fond of Dark Queen.

| Tuesday 2:00 PM | Dark Queen placed in the cat run by the parlour maid |

| Tuesday 5:15 PM | Police telephoned; Tupping immediately misdirects suspicion toward Luke |

---

## Luke's Innocence

Luke did not steal Dark Queen. He did not approach the cat run, the potting shed, or the back gate on Tuesday afternoon. He was visible in the front flower beds the entire time. He noticed two things of great significance — Mr. Tupping walking toward the cat run at 3:00 PM, which was unusual, and a motorcar idling on the back lane at 3:15 PM — but was too frightened to press these observations when questioned by the police. He should be cleared of all suspicion immediately.

---

## Can Dark Queen Be Recovered?

Possibly. The dealer who collected her will likely attempt to sell her quickly, given her distinctive appearance. She is black, flat-faced, and has a distinctive notch on her left ear from a childhood injury (Lady Candling can confirm this). If the detective can identify the dealer from Tupping's telephone records or contacts, and act swiftly, Dark Queen may still be found before she changes hands again. Lady Candling's insurance policy ensures she will not be left entirely without remedy, but the cat herself is, as Lady Candling says, irreplaceable.

---

## Conclusion

Mr. Tupping stole Dark Queen to pay an £80 gambling debt, having been refused a legitimate pay advance by Lady Candling the week before. He arranged the sale in advance, exploited his knowledge of the estate, framed an innocent young man, and lied to the police. The evidence against him — Miss Harmer's testimony, Mrs. Tupping's telephone account, the timing of the dealer's car, and his own false alibi — is conclusive.

Luke is innocent. Tupping is guilty. The cat may yet be saved.
